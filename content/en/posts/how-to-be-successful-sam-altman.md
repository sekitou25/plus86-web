Source: [How to Be Successful](https://blog.samaltman.com/how-to-be-successful). The excerpts below follow the order of my original notes; my own reflections appear between them.

## 1. Compound yourself

> Exponential curves are the key to wealth generation.

Pay attention to the growth rate, to the slope. Exponential growth matters.

Leverage is crucial: it lets the same amount of work produce more.

> There are many ways to get this leverage, such as capital, technology, brand, network effects, and managing people.

Before using leverage, have something worth amplifying. Become stronger first, then amplify it.

> adding another zero to whatever you define as your success metric

I believe in step changes in progress. They can hurt, but I trust that way of moving forward.

## 2. Have almost too much self-belief

> Managing your own morale—and your team’s morale—is one of the greatest challenges of most endeavors.

Believing in yourself matters because the road to a goal is long and difficult. You need conviction, almost to the point of talking yourself into it.

## 3. Learn to think independently

First-principles thinking.

> **“I will fail many times, and I will be really right once”**

## 4. Get good at “sales”

> you also have to be able to convince other people of what you believe.
>
> My best advice for communicating clearly is to first make sure your thinking is clear and then use plain, concise language.
>
> The best way to be good at sales is to genuinely believe in what you’re selling.

Talk face to face.

## 5. Make it easy to take risks

> Most people overestimate risk and underestimate reward.

This matters especially in trading. We accept a small possible loss in exchange for a larger potential profit.

> Look for small bets you can make where you lose 1x if you’re wrong but make 100x if it works. Then make a bigger bet in that direction.
>
> Keeping your life cheap and flexible for as long as you can is a powerful way to do this

We've talked about this. The extreme version of starting a company might mean living somewhere that only covers sleeping and showering, and spending all your time on the work. But the trade-off is obvious. I can't quite do that, though I can move closer to it.

## 6. Focus

> Once you have figured out what to do, be unstoppable about getting your small handful of priorities accomplished quickly.

## 7. Work hard

> **Extreme people get extreme results**

The earlier conditions still apply: choose work that gives you leverage and can grow exponentially. Working hard in a steel mill does not necessarily bring a good return.

> work stamina seems to be one of the biggest predictors of long-term success.

You need endurance over the long term.

## 8. Be bold

> I believe that it’s easier to do a hard startup than an easy startup. People want to be part of something exciting and feel that their work matters.

Things that look hard and things that look easy may be equally difficult once you actually do them. So choose something with room for imagination.

> Things that seem exciting to you will often seem exciting to other people too.

## 9. Be willful

> People have an enormous capacity to make things happen. **A combination of self-doubt, giving up too early, and not pushing hard enough prevents most people from ever reaching anywhere near their potential.**

Being willful lets us keep going longer, long enough for luck to go our way.

## 10. Be hard to compete with

> The best way to become difficult to compete with is to build up leverage.

How?

- A single skill is easy to replace. The intersection of two or three skills is harder to copy.
- Build assets that compound: work, a reputation, and relationships. Time is the substance of this moat.
- Put yourself in positive-sum situations. Create value, and the world can amplify your impact.

**The quality of your network shapes the opportunities you can reach. Take the initiative to connect with people you can learn from.**

## 11. Build a network

> An effective way to build a network is to help people as much as you can.

I've felt this in my own work. Being the person willing to help, even when it seems a little too accommodating, can pay off over a longer period.

> One of the best ways to build a network is to develop a reputation for really taking care of the people who work with you

## 12. You get rich by owning things

> You get truly rich by owning things that increase rapidly in value.
>
> The best way to make things that increase rapidly in value is by making things people want at scale.

What does that mean? Find a real need, then meet it in a way whose marginal cost approaches zero. That is the formula for rapidly growing value.

## 13. Be internally driven

This is easy to understand. More importantly, it is a way to keep life from becoming boring. An excerpt worth keeping:

> The most successful people I know are primarily internally driven; they do what they do to impress themselves and because they feel compelled to make something happen in the world. After you’ve made enough money to buy whatever you want and gotten enough social status that it stops being fun to get more, this is the only force I know of that will continue to drive you to higher levels of performance.
